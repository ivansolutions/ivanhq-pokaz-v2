      Routine reviewA map of your processes, with numbers.
      
I look at where you type the same thing twice, where you wait for someone else's answer and where mistakes are noticed too late. For every step: how many minutes, how often a day, how it ends. You get a map with three marks — the machine takes this now, this after a rebuild, and this is better left alone. The map stays with you, even if you stop there.
  • A step-by-step process map
  • Hours and money at each step
  • A list: what the machine takes over and what it doesn't

    

      Bots and assistantsTake orders and answer the usual questions.
      
Bots in Telegram, Slack, Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp or Discord: they take orders, answer the usual questions and pass everything else to a person — and that hand-over is logged, so you can see who took it and when. They run on n8n, a platform you can host on your own server.
  • A bot in your channel — and it says it's a bot
  • Answers you can edit yourself
  • Hand-over to a person, logged

    

      Connecting systemsEnter it once — it shows up everywhere it's needed.
      
Shop, CRM, email, spreadsheets, payments, messengers — connected so that a record is entered once and appears wherever it's needed. The repetitive part runs on n8n: you can see the whole connection and hand it over to someone else.
  • A connection you can see end to end
  • One entry instead of three
  • A log of every record

    

      AI agents with an emergency brakeThe agent asks, you answer with one button.
      
The boundary moves, and you move it. It's not a promise that the agent “won't”: without your approval it simply has no rights to email a customer, make a payment or change the CRM — that's how the keys are issued. It asks, and you answer with one button where you already are: Telegram, Slack or Microsoft Teams. A step that has proven itself on your own data again and again can run on its own. If results diverge, the brake comes back.
  • An agent for your tasks
  • A list of steps: where it asks, where it runs alone
  • An approval button before every such step

    

      Watchdog and reportsIf something breaks at 4 a.m., you hear it from me, not from a customer.
      
The watchdog checks an agreed list around the clock. If something breaks at four in the morning, you hear it from me, not from a customer — in Telegram, Slack or Microsoft Teams. What isn't on the list is clear in advance: we draw it up together and you keep it. Plus a short weekly report.
  • An alert when something breaks
  • A weekly report in plain words
  • A history of outages and fixes

    

      AI voice calls in preparation — not on offer yetThe voice says in its first sentence that it is a machine.
      
The voice says in its very first sentence that it is a machine and asks permission to record — without permission nothing is recorded or analysed. It answers incoming calls, handles the usual questions, takes down the request and passes on to a person what a person needs to decide. It also calls out — but only people who have agreed to it.
  • Incoming calls · call-backs only with consent
  • Call transcript — with permission
  • Request straight into your system

What I don't promise.

    
  • No “AI replaces people”.
    Automation takes the repetitive part. Decisions stay with people — and I write down exactly where the machine stops.
  • No magic without access.
    If the system has no way in, there's nothing to automate there. I'll tell you before you pay, not after.
  • No black box.
    Code, accounts and keys are yours. You can hand them to another developer and the work carries on without me.
  • No “the data is with me”.
    You decide where your data lives: on your machine, on your server or on a server in the country where it belongs.

    
A channel is also a place where data lives — choosing a channel means choosing a country. Want to stay in the EU? Then I'll build the channel there: your own chat or email, on your server or mine. Want Telegram, Slack or Microsoft Teams because your team is already there? That's a normal choice. But I'll tell you where the data goes before the work starts, not after.

Four letters — four steps.

    
  1. I
    Integration
    Your systems start talking to each other: shop, CRM, email, spreadsheets, payments, messengers.
  2. V
    Verification
    A record is checked before it moves on: required fields, amounts and bank details, duplicates. The list of checks is drawn up during the review and written down in advance. An error stops here, not in your report.
  3. A
    Automation
    The routine runs by itself: on a schedule, on an event or on a single command.
  4. N
    Notification
    The result goes where you already look: Telegram, Slack, Microsoft Teams, email or a simple board.
